# Producer Request Cards

Used on **Day 14 — Producer request to ticket**.

In real work, requests arrive vague. Someone says "it feels flat" and you have to turn that into work a developer can start. These cards are those vague requests.

## How to Use Them

1. Pick **one** card — roll a die, or choose the card that names your game's biggest weakness.
2. Have someone read the card aloud exactly as written, adding nothing.
3. You may ask up to **three questions**. They answer as a player, not as a developer. "I just didn't know where to go" is a good answer. "Add a waypoint arrow" is not.
4. Write the ticket: summary, acceptance criteria, files involved, not included.
5. Score it with the **Day 14 — Ticket Readiness** rubric in [Rubrics](Rubrics.md).
6. Deliver the ticket on Day 15.

The ticket must fit in one day of work and must not add a new game mode or break the scope rules.

## The Cards

**Card 1 — Lost at the Start**
"I opened it and I didn't know what I was supposed to do. I pressed some keys, something happened, and then I died."

**Card 2 — Feels Flat**
"When I score or hit something, it doesn't feel like anything. I can't tell if I did well."

**Card 3 — Didn't See It Coming**
"I keep losing and I don't know why. Something hits me and I never see it."

**Card 4 — Too Hard, Too Soon**
"The first ten seconds are brutal. I gave up after three tries."

**Card 5 — Too Easy**
"I won on my first go and wasn't really trying. I wouldn't play it twice."

**Card 6 — Why Play Again?**
"It was fine. But once I finished it, there's no reason to go back."

**Card 7 — What's My Status?**
"I never knew how I was doing. How much health did I have? How long was left? How close was I to winning?"

**Card 8 — Controls Fight Me**
"I know what I want to do but the character doesn't do it. It feels slippery, or late, or something."

**Card 9 — Nothing Changes**
"The first minute and the last minute feel exactly the same. It never builds."

**Card 10 — The End Is a Letdown**
"I won, I think? It just sort of stopped."

## Reading the Cards

Do not say these out loud when you are playing the requester. Use them to answer the three questions in character.

| Card | The real problem | A good ticket probably includes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | No teaching in the first 30 seconds | On-screen control hints, a safe opening moment, a visible first goal |
| 2 | No feedback on success | A visible reaction to scoring: flash, pop, number, shake |
| 3 | Threats aren't readable | Warning before danger, clearer shapes or colours, a hit reaction on the player |
| 4 | Difficulty starts too high | A gentler opening, difficulty that ramps over time |
| 5 | No challenge curve | Difficulty that rises, or a tighter win condition |
| 6 | No replay hook | Best score or best time shown, something that varies between runs |
| 7 | Missing HUD | On-screen health, timer, score or progress |
| 8 | Loose controls | Tuned speed and acceleration values, faster response to input |
| 9 | Flat pacing | Something that escalates: speed, count, a new obstacle type |
| 10 | Weak ending | A clear final moment and a win screen that shows what the player achieved |
