# 🎓 Intern and OJT Screening Playbook

## DEFINE
| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Playbook Name | "Intern and OJT Screening" |
| Purpose | Standardize how Engineering screens internship and OJT applicants before acceptance so capacity, school requirements, remote-work fit, technical readiness, and IP/legal protections are validated consistently. |
| Owner | Head of Engineering |
| Last Updated | 2026-07-30 |
| Review Cycle | Quarterly |

## Prerequisites
- [ ] The request is for **screening only**; onboarding and active supervision are handled separately after approval
- [ ] The screener is an **L4 Engineer or above**, or the **Head of Engineering**
- [ ] HR is available to run the **culture-fit screening call**
- [ ] Legal is available to review the university's **MOA/MOU** and draft the company contract on top of it when a candidate passes screening
- [ ] The candidate has shared or can obtain the school's required **OJT syllabus or requirement summary**
- [ ] The candidate can provide the required documents: **MOA/MOU**, **endorsement letter**, **resume**, and **NDA**
- [ ] The accepting supervisor understands the current capacity rule: **1 OJT per L4 Engineer and per L5 Engineer at a time**
- [ ] The screener understands that only **full remote** arrangements are supported
- [ ] The screener understands that only **training-project tasks** are allowed; no company production or existing product work may be assigned
- [ ] This playbook is open and visible before screening begins

## Step-by-Step Checklist
### Phase 1: Capture Inquiry and Route Ownership
1. **Receive** the internship or OJT inquiry from the candidate, school representative, employee referrer, or any L4-and-above engineering contact.
2. **Record** the candidate's basic intake details:
   - Full name
   - School and course
   - Expected OJT term
   - Required hours
   - Referral source
   - Whether the candidate is already a company employee
3. **Confirm** that the request is still early enough for review.
4. **Apply** the lead-time rule: applicants should contact Engineering **at least 3 months in advance** of the expected start date.
5. **IF** the expected start date is less than 3 months away, **THEN** continue only if the Head of Engineering explicitly allows an exception.
6. **Assign** a potential accepting supervisor who is **L4 or above**.
7. **Confirm** whether the potential accepting supervisor has available capacity under the **1 active OJT per L4/L5 engineer** rule.
- [ ] Intake details are complete enough to continue screening
- [ ] A potential accepting supervisor has been identified
- [ ] Capacity has been checked before deeper evaluation

### Phase 2: Check Eligibility, Capacity, and Program Fit
8. **Confirm** whether the candidate comes from an **IT-related course**.
9. **IF** the course is not IT-related, **THEN** require a **special-case justification** before proceeding.
10. **Validate** whether the special case is backed by a **character referral** from an existing staff member who has been with the company for **at least 1 year**.
11. **Reject** the application if it is non-IT-related and does not meet the special-case referral rule.
12. **Verify** that the school's required hours are between **160 and 240 hours**.
13. **Reject** the application if the required hours are below **160** or above **240**, unless the Head of Engineering approves an exception in writing.
14. **Confirm** that the candidate can work in a **full remote** setup for the entire program.
15. **Confirm** that the candidate understands the engineering OJT scope is limited to **training projects only**.
16. **IF** the candidate expects placement on an existing company product, **THEN** stop the process and clarify that Engineering does not accept OJTs for product-delivery work.
17. **Check** whether the candidate is already a current employee.
18. **IF** the candidate is already an employee who is entering the formal OJT period of college, **THEN** route the case to the Head of Engineering for **special dispensation review** before continuing.
- [ ] Course eligibility is validated
- [ ] Required hours fit the approved range
- [ ] Remote-work and training-only scope are accepted by the candidate
- [ ] Special dispensation cases are explicitly flagged

### Phase 3: Validate Required Documents and Legal Readiness
19. **Request** the required school and company documents:
   - MOA/MOU
   - Endorsement letter
   - Resume
   - NDA
20. **Review** the submission for completeness and readability before routing it further.
21. **Return** incomplete submissions to the candidate with a single consolidated list of missing items.
22. **Forward** the university's **MOA/MOU** to Legal for review once the document set is complete.
23. **Confirm** that Legal will draft the company contract on top of the university agreement before final acceptance.
24. **IF** Legal identifies unacceptable IP ownership, confidentiality, or contractual conflicts, **THEN** pause screening until the university or candidate resolves them.
25. **IF** the candidate is a current employee under special dispensation, **THEN** instruct Legal to include the employment-status change in the contract review:
   - Employee is moved to **OJT status**
   - Salary is removed
   - Allowance, if any, is governed by the new Legal-approved contract
- [ ] Required documents are complete
- [ ] Legal has the MOA/MOU for review
- [ ] IP and contract handling are addressed before approval

### Phase 4: Run HR and Technical Screening
26. **Schedule** the HR screening call to assess culture fit, professionalism, communication, and reliability.
27. **Document** the HR screening result as **pass**, **hold**, or **fail**.
28. **Stop** the process if HR marks the candidate as **fail**.
29. **Schedule** the technical assessment with the accepting supervisor who is **L4 or above**.
30. **Evaluate** whether the candidate can succeed in a remote, mentor-driven, training-project environment.
31. **Check** for baseline indicators that the candidate can follow instructions, communicate blockers, and complete scoped tasks with weekly reporting.
32. **Document** the technical assessment result as **pass**, **hold**, or **fail**.
33. **Stop** the process if the accepting supervisor marks the candidate as **fail**.
34. **IF** either HR or the accepting supervisor marks the candidate as **hold**, **THEN** resolve the missing concern before requesting final approval.
- [ ] HR culture-fit screening is documented
- [ ] Technical assessment is documented
- [ ] Only candidates with clear pass status move to approval

### Phase 5: Approve or Reject the Candidate
35. **Compile** the final screening packet for decision:
   - Intake summary
   - Eligibility check
   - Capacity confirmation
   - Required hours confirmation
   - Document completeness
   - Legal status
   - HR screening result
   - Technical assessment result
   - Assigned accepting supervisor
36. **Submit** the packet to the **Head of Engineering** for final approval.
37. **Approve** the candidate only after the Head of Engineering gives direct approval.
38. **Reject** the candidate if the Head of Engineering declines approval or if a blocker remains unresolved.
39. **Notify** the candidate of the result in writing.
40. **Hand off** approved candidates to the next workflow only after final approval, Legal alignment, and supervisor assignment are all complete.
41. **IF** the candidate is approved, **THEN** confirm the assigned mentor is the accepting **L4-and-above engineer** and the expected reporting cadence is **weekly minimum**.
42. **IF** the candidate is rejected, **THEN** close the intake with a brief reason that is professional and non-inflammatory.
- [ ] Head of Engineering decision is recorded
- [ ] Candidate result is communicated in writing
- [ ] Approved candidates have a named mentor and clean handoff status

## Common Mistakes & Fixes
| Common Mistake | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| Starting interviews before checking mentor capacity | Check the 1-active-OJT-per-L4/L5 rule during intake before deeper screening |
| Accepting non-IT applicants without a valid exception path | Require a staff character referral from someone with at least 1 year of tenure before continuing |
| Allowing hours outside the approved range | Enforce the 160-240 hour rule unless the Head of Engineering approves a documented exception |
| Treating OJTs as product contributors | Re-state early that only training-project tasks are allowed |
| Waiting until late-stage approval to review MOA/MOU IP terms | Route the university agreement to Legal as soon as the document set is complete |
| Approving an existing employee informally for OJT conversion | Require special dispensation from the Head of Engineering and a new Legal-reviewed contract |
| Moving forward after a vague HR or technical "hold" | Resolve the exact blocker first, then re-evaluate before asking for final approval |
| Giving verbal decisions without written follow-up | Communicate final approval or rejection in writing every time |

## Success Criteria
- [ ] Every applicant is screened by an L4-and-above engineer plus HR before final approval
- [ ] Capacity is validated against the 1-active-OJT-per-L4/L5 rule before acceptance
- [ ] Course fit, referral exceptions, required hours, and remote-work constraints are documented
- [ ] Required documents are complete before final approval is requested
- [ ] Legal has reviewed the MOA/MOU and IP-sensitive contract terms before acceptance
- [ ] Current-employee OJT cases go through Head of Engineering special dispensation review
- [ ] Only training-project placements are approved
- [ ] The Head of Engineering's final decision is recorded and communicated in writing
